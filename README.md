# Production of Biodiesel from Thevetia peruviana Seed Oil Using NaOH as Catalyst

## Project Overview

This project investigates the production and characterization of biodiesel from *Thevetia peruviana* seed oil using sodium hydroxide (NaOH) as a catalyst. The study also compares the FTIR characteristics of biodiesel produced using NaOH and potassium hydroxide (KOH), alongside the original seed oil.

The research explores the potential of *Thevetia peruviana* as a non-edible feedstock for renewable biodiesel production.

## Aim of the Study

To investigate the production of biodiesel from *Thevetia peruviana* seed oil through transesterification using NaOH as a catalyst.

## Objectives

- To produce biodiesel from *Thevetia peruviana* seed oil by transesterification.
- To characterize the biodiesel using Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy.
- To compare the FTIR results of biodiesel produced using NaOH and KOH with those of the raw seed oil.

## Materials and Methods

The study used *Thevetia peruviana* seed oil as the feedstock and methanol as the alcohol reactant. Sodium hydroxide (NaOH) and potassium hydroxide (KOH) were used as catalysts.

For the NaOH-catalyzed process, 100 g of seed oil was heated in a water bath maintained between 55°C and 60°C. A mixture of 15.82 g of methanol and 1 g of NaOH was added, and the reaction was maintained for two hours.

After the reaction, the mixture was allowed to separate into biodiesel and glycerol layers. The biodiesel was washed with warm distilled water, dried using anhydrous sodium sulphate, and filtered.

FTIR spectroscopy was used to identify the functional groups in the raw oil and the biodiesel samples.

## Results and Discussion

FTIR analysis identified characteristic functional groups in the raw seed oil and the biodiesel samples.

### NaOH-catalyzed biodiesel

The main absorption bands reported were:

- 3023.65 cm⁻¹: =C–H stretching
- 2933.47 cm⁻¹: Asymmetric C–H stretching
- 2859.13 cm⁻¹: Symmetric C–H stretching
- 1261.19 cm⁻¹: Ester C–O stretching

These bands indicate the presence of functional groups associated with fatty acid methyl esters.

### KOH-catalyzed biodiesel

The KOH-produced biodiesel showed characteristic absorption bands, including:

- 2922.2 cm⁻¹ and 2855.1 cm⁻¹: Aliphatic C–H stretching
- 1744.4 cm⁻¹: Ester carbonyl (C=O) stretching
- 1170.4 cm⁻¹: Ester C–O stretching
- 1013.8 cm⁻¹: C–O–C stretching

The KOH-produced biodiesel showed more pronounced spectral evidence of transesterification than the NaOH-produced sample under the conditions investigated.

## Conclusion

The study demonstrated the production of biodiesel from *Thevetia peruviana* seed oil using NaOH as a catalyst. FTIR analysis identified functional groups associated with biodiesel in the resulting samples.

The comparison of the FTIR spectra showed more pronounced evidence of transesterification in the KOH-catalyzed biodiesel than in the NaOH-catalyzed biodiesel. The findings highlight the potential of *Thevetia peruviana* seed oil as a non-edible feedstock for biodiesel production.

## Recommendations

- Further comparative studies should investigate homogeneous and heterogeneous catalysts.
- Engine performance and exhaust emission tests should be conducted using different biodiesel blends.
- Further research should explore ways to improve biodiesel oxidation stability and storage properties.
- Potential uses of glycerol and oil cake should be investigated.
